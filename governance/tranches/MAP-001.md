## Objective

Make the Atlas executable for readers rather than merely navigable.

The chapter teaches:

- multi-resolution reading;
- stable chapter identity;
- hard dependencies versus soft cross-links;
- epistemic labels;
- support routes;
- figure representation classes;
- source locks and documentary provenance;
- replay/audit boundaries;
- bounded allegory;
- goal-dependent reading routes.

## Six reader routes

The chapter includes:

1. linear foundations;
2. dependency-first;
3. concept/theme;
4. research-frontier;
5. proof/replay;
6. visual/Atlas-plate.

These are navigation strategies, not unique curricula.

## Allegory discipline

The atlas/map allegory is explicit:

- territory ↔ subject;
- map ↔ organized representation;
- legend ↔ notation/status;
- scale ↔ resolution;
- route ↔ reader-selected path;
- coordinates ↔ stable IDs;
- survey record ↔ provenance.

Limits are stated equally explicitly.

## Figure decision

No governed figure is added in v0.1.

The route table and documentary legend carry the chapter's core visual organization more directly than a decorative map plate would.

A later plate should be added only if it encodes actual dependency/provenance structure.
