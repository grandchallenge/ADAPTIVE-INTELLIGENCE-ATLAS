# ATLAS-CH-DYN-001 — Derivation Packet

## D1. Continuous autonomous dynamics

Let

\[
\dot x=f(x),
\qquad
x\in\mathbb R^d.
\]

Under conditions giving existence and uniqueness, the solution from initial state \(x_0\) defines a local flow

\[
\Phi_t(x_0).
\]

For an autonomous flow where both sides are defined,

\[
\Phi_0=\operatorname{id},
\]

and

\[
\Phi_{t+s}
=
\Phi_t\circ\Phi_s.
\]

The vector field \(f\) is the local evolution law.

A trajectory is one realized path.

The flow is the family of state transformations generated by the law.

## D2. Equilibria and linearization

An equilibrium satisfies

\[
f(x_\star)=0.
\]

Let

\[
x=x_\star+\delta.
\]

For differentiable \(f\),

\[
f(x_\star+\delta)
=
f(x_\star)
+
J_f(x_\star)\delta
+
O(\|\delta\|^2).
\]

Since \(f(x_\star)=0\),

\[
\dot\delta
=
J_f(x_\star)\delta
+
O(\|\delta\|^2).
\]

The linearized system is

\[
\dot\delta
=
A\delta,
\qquad
A=J_f(x_\star).
\]

If every eigenvalue of \(A\) has negative real part, the equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable; under standard smoothness assumptions this is in fact local exponential stability.

If at least one eigenvalue has positive real part, the equilibrium is unstable.

If an eigenvalue has zero real part, the linearization test can be inconclusive.

## D3. Zero-linearization counterexample pair

Consider

\[
\dot x=-x^3.
\]

At \(x_\star=0\),

\[
f'(0)=0.
\]

Now consider

\[
\dot x=x^3.
\]

Again,

\[
f'(0)=0.
\]

The same zero linearization therefore occurs in two systems with different nonlinear stability.

For the first system, trajectories move toward zero.

For the second, any nonzero state moves away in its own sign direction.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\lambda=0
\text{ does not decide nonlinear stability.}
}
\]

## D4. Lyapunov witness

For

\[
\dot x=-x^3,
\]

choose

\[
V(x)=\frac12x^2.
\]

Then

\[
V(x)>0
\]

for \(x\neq0\), and

\[
V(0)=0.
\]

Along trajectories,

\[
\dot V
=
\frac{dV}{dx}\dot x
=
x(-x^3)
=
-x^4.
\]

Hence

\[
\dot V<0
\]

for every \(x\neq0\).

Since \(V\) is radially unbounded on \(\mathbb R\), this scalar calibration system is globally asymptotically stable at the origin.

The chapter uses this as an exact example, not as a template that automatically applies to high-dimensional neural dynamics.

## D5. Discrete maps

For a discrete system

\[
x_{k+1}=F(x_k),
\]

a fixed point satisfies

\[
F(x_\star)=x_\star.
\]

Write

\[
x_k=x_\star+\delta_k.
\]

Then

\[
\delta_{k+1}
=
J_F(x_\star)\delta_k
+
O(\|\delta_k\|^2).
\]

If every eigenvalue of \(J_F(x_\star)\) lies strictly inside the unit circle, the fixed point is locally asymptotically stable.

If at least one eigenvalue has modulus greater than one, the fixed point is unstable.

Eigenvalues on the unit circle leave first-order classification potentially inconclusive.

The continuous-time criterion is based on real parts.

The discrete-time criterion is based on moduli.

They must not be conflated.

## D6. Supercritical pitchfork calibration

Consider

\[
\dot x
=
f(x;\mu)
=
\mu x-x^3.
\]

Equilibria satisfy

\[
x(\mu-x^2)=0.
\]

Therefore:

\[
x_\star=0
\]

for every \(\mu\), and for \(\mu>0\),

\[
x_\star=\pm\sqrt\mu.
\]

The scalar Jacobian is

\[
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
=
\mu-3x^2.
\]

### Origin

At \(x_\star=0\),

\[
\lambda_0=\mu.
\]

Thus:

- \(\mu<0\): locally asymptotically stable;
- \(\mu>0\): unstable;
- \(\mu=0\): nonhyperbolic.

At \(\mu=0\), the system becomes

\[
\dot x=-x^3,
\]

so the origin remains asymptotically stable despite the zero linearization.

### Outer branches

For

\[
x_\star=\pm\sqrt\mu,
\qquad
\mu>0,
\]

\[
\lambda_\pm
=
\mu-3\mu
=
-2\mu
<
0.
\]

Thus both outer equilibria are locally asymptotically stable.

The qualitative equilibrium structure changes at

\[
\mu=0.
\]

This is the canonical supercritical pitchfork calibration used by the chapter.

## D7. Bifurcation meaning

A bifurcation is a qualitative change in the dynamical structure as a parameter varies.

In the pitchfork example, the parameter \(\mu\) changes:

- the number of equilibria;
- the stability of the origin;
- the appearance of two new stable branches.

A bifurcation diagram is therefore not merely a graph of a function value.

It is a compact representation of invariant-state structure and stability across parameter regimes.

## D8. Hamiltonian dynamics

Let canonical phase coordinates be

\[
z=
\begin{pmatrix}
q\\
p
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Define the canonical symplectic matrix

\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&I\\
-I&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Hamilton's equations are

\[
\dot z
=
J\nabla H(z).
\]

Because

\[
J^\top=-J,
\]

we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dH}{dt}
&=
\nabla H(z)^\top\dot z\\
&=
\nabla H(z)^\top
J
\nabla H(z)\\
&=
0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The final equality follows because

\[
v^\top Jv=0
\]

for every \(v\) when \(J\) is skew-symmetric.

Thus an autonomous Hamiltonian is conserved along its exact flow.

## D9. Symplectic flow

For a Hamiltonian flow \(\Phi_t\), the tangent map satisfies

\[
D\Phi_t(z)^\top
J
D\Phi_t(z)
=
J.
\]

This is stronger than mere volume preservation.

Taking determinants gives

\[
\det(D\Phi_t)^2=1.
\]

For a continuous flow connected to the identity at \(t=0\),

\[
\det(D\Phi_t)=1.
\]

But the converse is not generally true in phase-space dimensions above two:

\[
\det M=1
\not\Rightarrow
M^\top J M=J.
\]

Symplectic structure preserves the canonical two-form, not merely volume.

## D10. Harmonic oscillator

Take

\[
H(q,p)
=
\frac12(q^2+p^2).
\]

Then

\[
\nabla H=
\begin{pmatrix}
q\\
p
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and Hamilton's equations are

\[
\dot q=p,
\qquad
\dot p=-q.
\]

In matrix form,

\[
\dot z
=
Az,
\qquad
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Since

\[
A^2=-I,
\]

the exact flow is

\[
\Phi_t
=
e^{tA}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos t&\sin t\\
-\sin t&\cos t
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

## D11. Exact symplectic and energy checks

Let

\[
M(t)=\Phi_t.
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
M(t)^\top J M(t)=J.
\]

Also,

\[
\det M(t)=1.
\]

For any

\[
z=(q,p),
\]

the transformed state is

\[
z_t=M(t)z.
\]

Because \(M(t)\) is orthogonal,

\[
q_t^2+p_t^2
=
q^2+p^2.
\]

Hence

\[
H(z_t)=H(z).
\]

The phase trajectories are circles.

## D12. What this chapter does not establish

The preceding structure does not imply:

- generic neural networks are Hamiltonian;
- generic optimizer dynamics are symplectic;
- local eigenvalue stability controls global behavior;
- asymptotic stability rules out transient amplification;
- a stable continuous system remains stable under arbitrary discretization.

Those require separate arguments.

The Non-normality keystone handles transient amplification.

The Numerical Intelligence chapter handles discretization and numerical stability.

The Split-Operator chapter handles composed evolution laws.

## Claim boundary

This packet establishes standard deterministic finite-dimensional dynamical-systems identities and exact calibration examples.

It supplies mathematical substrate for later Atlas interpretations of depth, optimization, routing, and adaptive computation; it does not itself establish that any particular machine-learning architecture realizes these structures.
